This site is automated and may contain errors

Software extracted every vote from the City Clerk's narrative minutes, and pattern-matching rules assigned the categories and summaries; no person or AI reads each item. The minutes are typed by hand, formatted inconsistently over thirteen years, and sometimes name the wrong person, so a vote can be misattributed, mislabeled, missing, or merged with another. Where a meeting's own motions make the right member unambiguous, we correct the clerk's slip; otherwise the item stays as written. We fix errors as we find them, so details may change. Treat this site as a starting point: every vote links to the official meeting record and the meeting video, which is the final word.

Source and scope

The City publishes minutes through Legistar, but Alameda's instance leaves the electronic vote-tally fields empty, so each roll call is parsed from the minutes text. The record runs from August 2013, the start of Legistar's archive, to the latest published minutes, and includes everyone who held a council seat in that span, not just current members. Each member's term dates come from their own roll-call appearances.

Only items with a recorded roll call count. A consent-calendar motion appears as one vote unless a member pulled an item, and procedural motions with a roll call are filed under Governance. When the clerk's stated tally disagrees with the names listed, the names are generally shown as written.

Categories

Each vote has one main category, assigned by rules from the item's title, and up to four dashed "also" tags where it clearly spans topics. Filters match either. The "also tagged" row holds cross-cutting topics (such as Labor & Workforce or Climate & Energy) and the place tag Alameda Point, which marks any item about the former Naval Air Station whatever its topic. Categories are navigation aids, not legal classifications.

Found a mistake?

Every vote has a "Report an error" link that opens a pre-filled issue on the project's GitHub page. Corrections are made in the scraper's rules, so one fix repairs every vote with the same pattern.

Absent vs. recused

"Absent" means the member wasn't recorded as present for that vote. "Recused" means the minutes say the member stepped out over a conflict of interest, which is declining to take part, not missing a meeting. Some absences are unrecorded recusals, so check the record before drawing conclusions. A member is counted only for votes held while seated: a vote before someone took office or after they left is never an absence.

Privacy

This site uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count page views and see which parts of the site are used. Cloudflare says the service sets no cookies and does not track visitors across sites. The project collects no accounts or personal details.

Draft minutes

The City Clerk's minutes become official when the Council approves them, usually at its next meeting or the one after. Votes from meetings whose minutes are still waiting for approval carry a "draft minutes" label and may be corrected. The label comes off at the next update after the approval.

Videos and transcripts

Every vote links to the City's recording of its meeting. Where the item's start could be found, "watch item" opens the video there: for 2013 to early 2021 from the City's own agenda index, and for 2025 on from where the chair or clerk names the item in the video's captions. A consent item that was approved with the others links to where the consent calendar was taken up. These links are matched automatically and can land a minute or two off, so confirm the item on screen. Other votes link to the whole meeting. A few meetings have two recordings, and a few have none.

"Transcript" opens YouTube's automatic captions of the meeting, scrolled to the item. Captions exist for the City's YouTube videos from 2025 (and one from 2024); there are none for earlier meetings. They are machine-generated, so names, numbers and legal terms are often wrong and speakers are not identified. They are not the official record.

Alameda Post links

A vote links to an Alameda Post news story when one discussing the item ran within about a week of the meeting; these matches are automatic and sometimes wrong. Other votes from 2022 on offer an expandable list of the Post's "City Council" stories from around that meeting (they cover the meeting generally, not necessarily the item), or a keyword search when there are none. The Post's archive is nearly empty before 2022, so earlier votes have no Post link.

Reading the numbers fairly

A high dissent rate isn't good or bad by itself: it can reflect principled disagreement, a different era's priorities, or a council that rarely splits. For context, read the staff report or watch the meeting.
